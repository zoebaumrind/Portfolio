# Job-search guide: strategy, insights, and cultural-analyst roles in media and entertainment

Written for Zoe Baumrind, Binghamton University Class of 2027. This is the playbook that goes with the portfolio site.

The job you described ("presenting to big companies like the NYT what's trending online and how to use it") is real. It goes by several names, it lives in more places than you'd expect, and the hiring bar is a portfolio of actual analysis, which is exactly what the Culture Lab is for.

---

## 1. What the job is actually called

Search for these titles. Entry-level versions usually carry "Junior," "Associate," "Coordinator," or "Analyst."

**Closest match to what you described**

- Cultural Strategist / Culture Strategist
- Trends Analyst / Trend Researcher / Trends & Insights Analyst
- Cultural Insights Analyst / Cultural Intelligence Analyst
- Strategist (at creative agencies, "strategist" usually means cultural and consumer strategy)
- Foresight Analyst / Futures Analyst (trend forecasting firms)
- Creative Strategist (at platforms like TikTok, Meta, Snap, Pinterest: you present trend and creative insights to advertisers)

**Adjacent titles that use the same skills**

- Strategic Planner / Account Planner / Junior Planner (advertising agencies)
- Consumer Insights Analyst / Audience Insights Analyst
- Social Listening Analyst / Social Intelligence Analyst
- Research Analyst (media research, entertainment research)
- Audience Development Associate (publishers, streamers)
- Brand Strategist / Brand Planner
- Marketing Analyst / Media Analyst
- Content Strategist / Editorial Strategist
- Associate Consultant (media and entertainment practice)

**LinkedIn search strings to reuse**

```
("cultural strategist" OR "trends analyst" OR "cultural insights" OR "strategist") AND (entertainment OR media OR "pop culture")
("insights analyst" OR "audience insights" OR "social listening") AND (streaming OR publisher OR entertainment)
("junior strategist" OR "associate strategist" OR "strategy coordinator") AND (agency OR advertising)
("creative strategist" OR "client solutions") AND (TikTok OR Snap OR Pinterest OR Meta)
```

Set alerts on each string. Filter to "Entry level" and "Associate," and also run them without the filter, because many junior strategy roles are mislabeled as mid-level.

## 5. How to stand out (this is what the portfolio is for)

Hiring managers for these roles do not care much about GPA. They care whether you can spot something real, explain why it matters, and tell them what to do. Prove it publicly.

1. **Publish a Culture Lab brief every two weeks.** One page. Signal, why now, who should care, the play, watch-outs. Post it on LinkedIn and link to the site. After three months you'll have a body of work most applicants don't.
2. **Pick targets and write briefs on them.** Applying to the NYT? Write "Three things the NYT should do on TikTok this quarter." Send it with the application. This is the single most effective move in this field.
3. **Keep the analytics visible.** Business Analytics is your differentiator against pure "culture" candidates. Every brief should have at least one chart, table, or number you pulled yourself (Google Trends, platform data, Nielsen, chart positions).
4. **Build a trend radar and keep it current.** The table on the Culture Lab page is a running list; a recruiter who sees it updated last week knows you actually do this.
5. **Say the words they search for.** "Cultural strategy," "consumer insights," "social listening," "trend research," and "audience insights" should appear on your LinkedIn headline and About section.
6. **Turn your interests into credentials.** "Reality TV" on a resume is a hobby; "second-screen fandom behavior" in a brief is expertise.
7. **Use the standing offer.** The site promises a one-page brief on any brand within a week. Honor it. It converts conversations into interviews.
